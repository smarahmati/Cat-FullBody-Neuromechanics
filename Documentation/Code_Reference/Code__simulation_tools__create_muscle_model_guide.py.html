  86 <article><h3>1. Motor command</h3><p>u(t), from 0 to 1, is read from the fixed excitation table. The same prescribed cycle is repeated throughout the forward calculation.</p></article>
  87 <article><h3>2. Activation dynamics</h3><p>Activation a(t) rises and falls with finite activation/deactivation time constants. It does not instantaneously become equal to excitation.</p></article>
  88 <article><h3>3. Mechanical equilibrium</h3><p>The elastic solver finds fiber length such that tendon tension balances active, passive and damping contributions. Tendon length changes as the total path and fiber length change.</p></article>
  89 <article><h3>4. Coupled movement</h3><p>The resulting tendon force acts along the muscle path. Its moment arms convert it to joint loading. All four limbs, the trunk and paw contacts participate in the coupled forward dynamics.</p></article></div>

  98 <p class="parameter-note"><strong>Two implementation details:</strong> Passive fiber force is ON in all channels. It can nevertheless be zero when a fiber is shorter than its passive stretch threshold. The tendon is elastic in all channels. Also, F0 is the maximum isometric muscle force; applying an additional cos α factor to the force would double-project it. The implementation uses α in the length partition and holds it constant.</p>
  99 <h2>Passive fiber force and its sources</h2>
 100 <p>The current model uses the passive exponential in Prilutsky et al. (2016), Table 10.3, and Rahmati et al. (2025), Table 3, with coefficients 0.0075 and 11.6. It is enabled in all limb channels, inside the elastic force-equilibrium solve. This constitutive law is part of the muscle equilibrium equations for every limb.</p>
 101 <p>Zeroing compressive values and limiting the exponent for overflow protection are explicit adaptations. Extending one normalized law to added channels is an assumption, not muscle-specific passive calibration. The passive curve coefficients are taken from the cited publications; they are not fitted to this walking trial. <a href="passive_fiber_mechanics.html">Passive-force equation and stretch verification</a>.</p>

 118 <h2>If excitation becomes zero, does force vanish immediately?</h2>
 119 <p>No. Activation first decays according to its time constant. Stretched tendon can retain tension, and passive fiber force can remain. The evolving muscle–tendon and skeletal states determine how force changes. The model therefore distinguishes zero excitation, zero activation and zero tendon force.</p>
